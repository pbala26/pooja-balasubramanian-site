Development Economics

2017–2020, University of Göttingen

Hero image placeholder
Lecturer Prof. Dr. Marcela Ibanez  · Tutorials  Dr. Pooja Balasubramanian

In this course students will learn about the politics around poverty in developing countries, including its measurement and key determinants. They can explain the linkages between poverty, hunger, gender inequality, and fertility. They can analyze how market failures in markets for land, labor, capital and insurance can trap households in poverty, and derive appropriate policy recommendations to tackle these poverty traps. They can use regression analysis and impact evaluation methods to assess determinants of poverty and ways to overcome it.

Textbook

The main text book for this lecture is Debraj Ray (1998). Development Economics. Princeton University Press.

We also discuss material from Allan De Janvry and Elizabeth Sadoulet’s book, Development economics: Theory and practice.

Readings marked with an asterisk (*) below are required; the rest are recommended.

Exam and credits

There will be a 90 minute exam at the end of the course. Students should demonstrate a good understanding of poverty, its measurement and determinants in developing countries. They should also be able to critically present theories and models of market failures for land, labor, capital and insurance markets that can trap households in poverty. Students have to demonstrate that they are able to interpret empirical results that relate to these models, and are able to draw relevant policy conclusions coming out of these models and empirical assessments.

Students will earn 6 credits for the course.
