In this course students will learn about the politics around poverty in developing countries, including its measurement and key determinants. They can explain the linkages between poverty, hunger, gender inequality, and fertility. They can analyze how market failures in markets for land, labor, capital and insurance can trap households in poverty, and derive appropriate policy recommendations to tackle these poverty traps. They can use regression analysis and impact evaluation methods to assess determinants of poverty and ways to overcome it.

Textbook

The main text book for this lecture is Debraj Ray (1998). Development Economics. Princeton University Press.

We also discuss material from Allan De Janvry and Elizabeth Sadoulet’s book, Development economics: Theory and practice.

Readings marked with an asterisk (*) below are required; the rest are recommended.

Exam and credits

There will be a 90 minute exam at the end of the course. Students should demonstrate a good understanding of poverty, its measurement and determinants in developing countries. They should also be able to critically present theories and models of market failures for land, labor, capital and insurance markets that can trap households in poverty. Students have to demonstrate that they are able to interpret empirical results that relate to these models, and are able to draw relevant policy conclusions coming out of these models and empirical assessments.

Students will earn 6 credits for the course.

Course outline

I. Poverty measurement and analysis

  • Definition of poverty
  • Income and non-income poverty
  • Identification and aggregation issues
  • Robustness checks & sensitivity analysis

Reading:

  • Ray, Chapter 8*
  • Dreze & Srinivasan (1997). Widowhood and Poverty in Rural India. Journal of Development Economics 54(2): 217–234.*
  • Lanjouw (2005). Adjusting for differences in needs and economies of scale in the measurement of poverty in Morocco. World Bank Working Paper.
  • Klasen, Lechtenfeld & Povel (2015). A feminization of vulnerability? Female headship, poverty and vulnerability in Thailand and Vietnam. World Development 71: 36–53.

II. Manifestations and correlates of poverty

2. Poverty and fertility

  • Linkages between fertility and poverty
  • Determinants of the ‘supply’ of children
  • Determinants of the ‘demand’ for children
  • The role of family planning policies

Readings:

  • Ray, Chapters 9.1–9.3*
  • De Janvry & Sadoulet (2015). Development Economics: Theory and Practice. Routledge, Chapter 11.
  • Ashraf, Field & Lee (2014). Household bargaining and excess fertility: an experimental study in Zambia. The American Economic Review 104(7): 2210–2237.
  • Bongaarts & Sinding (2011). Population policy in transition in the developing world. Science 333(6042): 574–576.
  • May (2012). World population policies: Their origin, evolution, and impact. Springer.

3. Migration and unemployment

  • Migration, informality, and urban unemployment
  • The Harris-Todaro model

Readings:

  • Ray, Chapter 10*
  • Kingdon & Knight (2006). How flexible are wages in response to local unemployment in South Africa? Industrial and Labor Relations Review 59: 471–495.
  • Williamson (1998). Migration and Urbanization. Handbook of Development Economics, Vol. 1, ch. 11.

III. Vicious cycles of poverty in rural areas

4. Poverty and access to land

  • Land distribution
  • Share-cropping: efficiency and risk issues
  • Land markets
  • Land reforms and land rights

Readings:

  • Ray, Chapters 10, 12*
  • Binswanger-Mkhize, Bourguignon & van den Brink (eds.) (2009). Agricultural land redistribution: toward greater consensus. World Bank, Chapter 1* and Chapter 15*

6. Insurance markets

  • How risk affects poverty outcomes
  • Potential and limits of insurance
  • Partial insurance
  • Self-insurance
  • Credit and insurance

Readings:

  • Ray, Chapter 15*

  • Causes & prevention of famines

  • Nutritional efficiency wages & nutrition-based poverty traps
  • Conceptual and empirical issues

Readings:

9. Gender inequality

  • The extent of gender inequality

Readings:

  • De Janvry & Sadoulet (2015). Development Economics: Theory and Practice. Routledge, Chapter 14.*
